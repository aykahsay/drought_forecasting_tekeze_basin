## 1. The 3-Phase Climate Chain Reaction Across Time and Space

Drought in the rugged, semi-arid terrain of Yechila Abergelle is not an unpredictable isolated event. It is the terminal local expression of an inter-basin atmospheric teleconnection operating on a **3-to-6 month physical sequence**:

```
[Phase 1: Jan – Mar]                 [Phase 2: Apr – May]                [Phase 3: Jun – Aug]

 ◄────────────────────── 3-to-6 Month Physical Predictive Lead Time ──────────────────────►
```

### Physical Dynamics of the Three Sequential Phases:

1. **Phase 1: The Ocean Signal (3–6 Months Ahead: January – March)**
   - Sea Surface Temperatures (SST) spike in the central-eastern equatorial Pacific (Niño 3.4 region: $5^\circ\text{N}-5^\circ\text{S}, 170^\circ\text{W}-120^\circ\text{W}$) or the Western Indian Ocean (+IOD).
   - This thermal perturbation alters tropical convection, suppressing the ascending branch of the Walker circulation over the Horn of Africa.

2. **Phase 2: The Basin Response (0–2 Months Ahead: April – May)**
   - The anomalous atmospheric heating pattern displaces the **Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)** southward, delaying its northward migration over the Horn of Africa.
   - This suppresses the pre-monsoon *Belg* rains and weakens the Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ).
   - Soil moisture in the upper Tekeze Basin catchment is depleted before summer, pre-conditioning the watershed for severe hydrological drought.

3. **Phase 3: Localized Woreda Impact (Primary Season: June – August)**
   - During the primary *Kiremt* monsoon (June to August), southwest moisture-bearing winds from the Congo Basin and Gulf of Guinea fail to penetrate the lower elevations of Yechila Abergelle.
   - Seasonal rainfall drops by 30% to 55%, and Vertisol root-zone moisture collapses below the permanent wilting point ($< 0.05\text{ m}^3/\text{m}^3$), producing widespread crop failure during the critical flowering stage of sorghum (*Mashilla*) and sesame (*Selit*).

### Key Empirical Findings:

1. **The 3-to-4 Year ENSO Coherence Band (Panel A Upper Zone):**
   - The Y-axis represents the wavelet cycle period (in months) from 3 to 42 months, and the X-axis tracks historical time.

2. **Phase Alignment Vectors ($\leftarrow$ Anti-Phase Arrows):**
   - In wavelet coherence, the phase difference is represented by directional arrows:
     * **Rightward arrows ($\rightarrow$):** In-phase relationship ($0^\circ$).
     * **Leftward arrows ($\leftarrow$):** Anti-phase relationship ($180^\circ$, or $\pm \pi$).
   - Throughout the coherent ENSO frequency band, the vectors point **consistently leftward ($\leftarrow$) with a downward inclination (phase angle $\approx -113^\circ$)**.
   - **Physical Meaning:** Positive sea surface temperature anomalies in the Pacific (El Niño) drive negative precipitation anomalies (drought) in Yechila Abergelle and the Tekeze Basin.

3. **High-Frequency Decoupling (12-Month Monsoon Band):**
   - In the annual 12-month monsoon band (blue dashed line), coherence drops to **$R^2 \approx 0.26$** (cool blue region).
   - This demonstrates that while individual local rain events contain stochastic weather noise, the **multi-year drought regime** is locked to the global ocean engine.

4. **Cone of Influence (COI):**
   - The black curve and hatched gray shading delineate the Cone of Influence (COI), where edge effects from finite time series become non-negligible. High coherence remains statistically robust within the unshaded cone.
